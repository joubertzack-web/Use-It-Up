Purpose
-------
This document records the current top five recipe-source candidates for
Use It Up. It is a Phase 0 research document, not a final licensing approval.

IMPORTANT PRODUCT PRINCIPLE
---------------------------
Use It Up is NOT exclusively a Southern/Midwestern/cowboy-ranch recipe app.

Those preferences are useful as an initial real-world test case, but the
commercial product must support broad culinary variety. The source strategy
therefore needs to balance:

- Strong initial American home-cooking coverage
- Broad cuisine variety
- Open/reusable content
- Clear licensing
- Structured recipe data where possible
- Long-term commercial viability
- Attribution and provenance tracking


TOP 5 CANDIDATES
================

1. WIKIBOOKS COOKBOOK
---------------------
Role:
Primary open recipe-source candidate.

Why it is strong:
- Several thousand user-contributed recipes.
- Broad coverage across cultures and cuisines.
- Includes Southern U.S., Midwestern/North American, Texas, Cajun,
  and other regional material.
- Recipe content is available under Creative Commons
  Attribution-ShareAlike, subject to the applicable page/license terms.
- The project is actively maintained.

Relevant examples:
- Biscuits and Gravy
- Chicken-Fried Steak
- Country Fried Steak and Gravy
- Fried Chicken
- Skillet Cornbread
- Southern Cornbread
- Grits
- Collard Greens
- Fried Okra
- Chicken Pot Pie

Primary reference:
https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Cookbook:Recipes

License reference:
https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Cookbook:Southern_Cuisine

Status:
HIGH PRIORITY — FURTHER LICENSE/INGESTION REVIEW REQUIRED


2. LIBRARY OF CONGRESS / PUBLIC-DOMAIN COOKBOOKS
------------------------------------------------
Role:
Historical and heritage recipe source.

Why it is strong:
- Contains digitized historical American cookbooks.
- Public-domain works can provide legally reusable historical recipes.
- Particularly valuable for traditional Southern and regional American
  cooking.
- Provides authoritative provenance and bibliographic information.

Example:
The Southern Cookbook; A Manual of Cooking and List of Menus (1912).

The Library of Congress identifies the books in the referenced collection
as public domain and free to use and reuse.

Primary reference:
https://www.loc.gov/item/12026875/

Status:
HIGH PRIORITY — STRONG SUPPLEMENTARY SOURCE


3. JSON COOKBOOK
----------------
Repository:
micahcochran/json-cookbook

Role:
Structured/open recipe dataset candidate.

Why it is interesting:
- Recipes are provided in JSON.
- Uses the schema.org/Recipe structure.
- The project documents source licensing.
- Its included recipes come from sources identified as Creative Commons
  or Public Domain.
- Individual recipe license metadata is preserved.

Repository:
https://github.com/micahcochran/json-cookbook

Status:
HIGH PRIORITY FOR TECHNICAL EVALUATION


4. RECIPEDB
-----------
Role:
Large structured recipe-database candidate.

Why it is interesting:
- Large structured recipe collection.
- Provides downloadable data.
- Useful as a possible research/benchmark source.
- Could be valuable for ingredient normalization, recipe matching,
  and recommendation-system development.

CRITICAL LICENSE NOTE:
RecipeDB data is distributed under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0.

That NonCommercial restriction makes it unsuitable to automatically
treat RecipeDB as a commercial production data source.

It may still be useful for research, prototyping, benchmarking, or
other uses permitted by its license, but commercial incorporation
requires separate legal review/permission.

Primary reference:
https://cosylab.iiitd.edu.in/recipedb/FAQ

Status:
RESEARCH / BENCHMARK CANDIDATE — COMMERCIAL USE RESTRICTED


5. USER / FAMILY-CREATED RECIPES
-------------------------------
Role:
First-class native recipe source.

Why it is important:
- Users can enter their own recipes.
- Family recipes can be preserved digitally.
- No dependence on external recipe databases for personal recipes.
- Provides a foundation for community/user-generated content.
- Can eventually become a major source of recipe variety.

Examples:
- Grandma's recipes
- Family recipes
- Personal recipes
- User-created variations
- Community-contributed recipes

Important:
User-submitted content must have explicit ownership/usage terms in the
application's Privacy & Data Handling and Terms/Legal documentation.

Status:
CORE PRODUCT SOURCE


SECONDARY CANDIDATE / WATCH LIST
================================

RECIPE COMMONS
-------------
Recipe Commons is an emerging open initiative built around an open
recipe format. It describes recipes as portable Markdown files with
per-recipe Creative Commons licensing and states that the project itself
is dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0.

It is currently described as Early Alpha, so it should be monitored rather
than automatically elevated into the initial five-source strategy.

Reference:
https://recipecommons.org/

Status:
WATCH / FUTURE EVALUATION


SOURCE STRATEGY PRINCIPLES
==========================

1. Publicly viewable does NOT automatically mean reusable.

2. Every externally sourced recipe must have a documented legal basis
   before being incorporated into the product.

3. Preserve provenance:
   - Original source
   - Author/creator where available
   - License
   - License URL
   - Source URL
   - Import date
   - Modifications, if applicable

4. Do not assume that an entire recipe website/database can be copied
   simply because individual recipes are publicly accessible.

5. Commercial viability matters from the beginning.

6. NonCommercial licenses must be clearly separated from production
   sources that may eventually support a paid/commercial product.

7. The application should be designed to support multiple recipe sources,
   not become dependent on a single external provider.

8. Source diversity is a product requirement:
   Southern, Midwestern, American comfort food, international cuisines,
   baking, vegetarian, healthy meals, desserts, quick meals, etc.

9. Initial testing may emphasize Southern/Midwestern home cooking, but
   the production recipe ecosystem must not be limited to those cuisines.
